The timeline

History moves forward. Discovery does not have to.

Book One begins near the outcome. Later books return to the choices that made it possible.

Confirmed story information, developing ideas, and unanswered questions are labeled separately.

Two maps

Two different maps

Two maps, two questions. The chronology asks: what caused what? The revelation order asks: what does the reader know, suspect, and get wrong at each moment? They are not the same map, and the story keeps them separate.

The broad order is settled. The details are not: exact dates, how long stages overlap, where the Disclosure Wars fall, and several characters' ages. One warning belongs on the map itself. The leaders' program runs for thousands of years. The Samuel and Konrad conflict is much shorter, and must not be stretched to fill it.

Spoilers: the chronological sequence
  • The leaders create the environment. They build the colonization world and develop the Luminai inside it. The program runs for thousands of years.
  • Samuel enters containment early. His early entry gives him seniority inside the later hierarchy. His exact entry sequence still needs reconciling.
  • Konrad's conquest and defeat. His conquest produces the Great War, and his defeat. His Daemon learns through the war.
  • The first run. Konrad sends his son Aiden into the same colonization process Sylvan later enters. The contained criminals hold the advantage. Aiden breaks a few years in, joins them, and reports the opposite: that he resisted and won his group its own ground. Konrad restarts his breeding program on that report, and Samuel takes control of the program through it.
  • The offer. The primary leaders offer Konrad's group permanent control of the largest empire if it joins Samuel and takes Sylvan before his forty-second birthday. The planet is not offered. The process is not offered.
  • The lie. Samuel lies about what Sylvan and the new Luminai really are. Konrad stays because the lie makes the prize look reachable: his experienced Daemon, his old model of domination.
  • The reactivation. Samuel performs weakness. He offers false freedom. He lets Konrad's Daemon check the display and approve it. Konrad authorizes the restart, inside Samuel's structure.
  • The capture. Samuel captures the group's institutions, its succession, its interpretation of events, and its generations. Konrad ends up alone inside a fantasy of future victory.
  • Two planets. Samuel, Konrad, and the older generation stay on the previous planet. George acts directly on the newer one, where Sylvan and Orzai meet, resist manufactured distrust, and build their bonds.
  • The bonds hold. Sylvan and Orzai protect their resources, keep cooperating on their own terms, and stay anchored in reality and ethics while they complete separate bonds.
  • The deadline passes. Sylvan reaches forty-two, untaken. The imperial route closes for good. Cooperation, for control of a city, is the only constructive path left to the older group.
  • The assessment. The assessment certifies their completed bonds, their chosen partnership, their conduct, and their eligibility for placement.
  • The circle sees it. Konrad's inner circle compares his counterfeit commands with the world outside, and realizes he never held control.
  • One throne, promised once. Samuel promises George absolute, godlike authority, in secret. George believes his promise. Aiden, who joined Samuel's side in his own run of the process, helps keep George believing. The power staged around George looks real while Sylvan is at his weakest.
  • The exposure. Sylvan begins exposing Samuel in public. The weapon fails. George learns what his promise was worth. Aiden is exposed sabotaging Sylvan to bury the breeding scandal and his own lies. One throne, promised once, shows one believer and one accomplice.
  • The tactic becomes evidence. Samuel keeps trying to aim George at Aiden. The repetition itself becomes live evidence that he is still giving commands. When the exposure lands, Samuel blames Aiden and George for everything and puts his unauthorized heir, Samuel Jr., forward to take the groups.
  • The recognition. The trapped leaders recognize Samuel as the deceiver, and have to choose how to fight their way out. Samuel Jr. turns toward Sylvan. Konrad begins the disclosure he committed to.
  • The refusal. Sylvan refuses Samuel's false bargain. The process formally separates Samuel from Sylvan, and takes away Samuel's process-provided attack systems.
  • The last allocation. Samuel receives $15 million to run his plan on his own. He tries to keep the war alive and stage an apocalyptic ending that makes him a living god.
  • Zero. The obsession burns through the money in days. The zero balance proves the failure is real, and triggers processing.
  • Processing. Samuel's Daemon is deleted. He enters a stasis-like coma. Biological synthetics move him to the older contained leaders, and his complete conduct is presented.
  • Konrad faces the record. Konrad is pulled out of the story environment that held him for a century, into the connected reality of Samuel's campaign. He still has influence, so his final moves matter.
  • Acceptance. Orzai lets the process carry the exposure. She accepts the completed result first, and leads Sylvan into their final decisions.
  • Departure. Sylvan and Orzai begin their exploratory period. They travel, hear offers from other Houses, and choose the country where they will build their own, using what they preserved.

Avoid a predetermined procession

Knowing the broad ending does not spoil the books. It does not say who helped, who refused, what was misunderstood, or what was lost. The later books still need local goals that matter on their own. Their order stays flexible on purpose: development will choose whichever return to the past is most entertaining, and most necessary.
